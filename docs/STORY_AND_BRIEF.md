## English — A Story That Began at a White House

My story begins in a village in Bihar, at a white house. Red along the lower walls, a veranda in front, trees all around—the home I left each morning for school. Looking at its photograph brings back an entire world: the heat, the road to school, my friends' voices, and the comfort of coming home.

I'm Affan Ahmad. Today, my world includes Java, Spring Boot, React, and software systems. The opening scene of this story takes us back to a school bag, the fierce Bihar sun, and a school built from bamboo.

My earliest school years, starting in nursery, were spent at **New Public School**. It was a simple village school, built from bamboo and a little worn down. Getting to school and back in the summer heat was a significant part of each day. Back then, thinking about the future meant moving up to the next class. Who had their whole career planned out?

That was where I began learning to read and write. My world was small, and every new discovery felt big. Today, whenever I sit down to understand a new tool or programming concept, it feels like that same process of learning is still going.

Around Class 4, the next chapter of my schooling began at **Holy Mission Public School**. As the subjects grew, so did my circle of friends. Those years, through Class 10, were filled with studying, fun, and people who became an important part of everyday life.

One of them was **Shubhangi**.

She was a lovely, kind friend, and over time we became best friends. That friendship has its own place in my memories of school. With some people, conversation comes easily, time feels well spent, and having them around makes the day a little better.

After Class 10, our paths separated. The distance grew, and eventually our friendship came to an end. It remains a fond part of that chapter of my life. Growing up also taught me that a good friendship can keep its place in your memories even when it doesn't last forever.

Outside school, I had another favourite world: **cricket**.

Playing with my village friends was one of the best parts of childhood. I was good at cricket, and I gave each match everything I had. Every run mattered, every wicket brought excitement, and a close match felt like our very own World Cup. Cricket still brings out my competitive side.

We had a lot of fun together. Looking back, I can see how much I have always enjoyed spending time with people. Being part of a team, supporting each other, and putting our energy into something together have stayed with me ever since.

For Classes 11 and 12, I attended **Dr. Lohiya Karpoori Visheshwar Das College in Samastipur**. Between **2019 and 2021**, I studied Physics, Chemistry, and Mathematics. COVID arrived during that period, bringing uncertainty to both my studies and my plans for what came next.

**In 2021, I took a break.** I then went to Kolkata to learn civil architecture design.

Learning something different in a new city became an interesting turn in my journey. I enjoyed seeing an idea take shape through design. Looking back, I realise that I already liked making things. I explored design first, and later that interest grew deeper through software development.

**My Bhopal chapter began in October 2022.** I started my B.Tech in Computer Science Engineering at the **Sagar Institute of Research and Technology, SIRT**.

A new city, a new campus, new subjects, and a fresh start. As I learned to code, I began to feel that I could build my own ideas. Understanding Java's logic, bringing something to life on a screen, and connecting the frontend to the backend became enjoyable.

And during those college years, I found my **Fab Four: Deeksha, Ayan, Banshita, and me**.

Deeksha became a very sweet and close best friend. With Ayan and Banshita, the four of us had a world of our own. Their company gave my Bhopal chapter a sense of belonging. Between studying and thinking about the future, there was always room for laughter with friends.

**Travelling with friends to take part in hackathons** became another special part of college. Journeys to **Manipal University Jaipur** and **ABV-IIITM Gwalior** brought together the excitement of travelling and the challenge of building something that worked.

At a hackathon, you have to turn an idea into a working solution. Time is limited, problems come up, and the team has to make decisions together. I enjoyed that hands-on way of learning. Becoming a **finalist at the national-level hackathon at ABV-IIITM Gwalior in October 2024** was a proud moment. In **October 2025, I was a winner in the Meshmerize zonal round of IIT Bombay Techfest**.

Alongside those achievements, the journeys I made with friends remain just as vivid in my memory.

And when it was time for a break, there was the pleasure of going somewhere together. **Our trip to Pachmarhi**, the hills, and the company of friends gave this part of my story a different mood. For me, the best part of a trip like that is the time I get with people I care about. A good place and good company can be more than enough.

By the later part of college, coding had become a serious part of my everyday life. **Between September and November 2025, I began freelancing.** During that period, I worked on the backend of **MedexJob.com**, a healthcare job portal.

I had real responsibility for my decisions. Authentication, job searches and application flows, notifications, and communication between services all had to come together as a working system. I used **Java, Spring Boot, JWT, and Apache Kafka** to meet real requirements. I tested APIs, handled the database and deployment, and prepared the backend for frontend integration.

Seeing something I had built go live was a special feeling. It also showed me how much testing, security, and attention to small details matter when delivering software.

**In December 2025, I joined my first company, CloudNexus, as a Software Development Engineer 1.** My professional life began while I was still in the final phase of my B.Tech.

**Aarya and Anshika** also became part of this chapter. A new professional environment brought new people into my life, and my world began to grow beyond my college circle.

**Since February 2026, I have been working on Java backend development for SanMar's Customer Order Services project.** My work includes Spring Boot microservices, REST APIs, order and shipment workflows, asynchronous listeners, and integrations. Deploying services on **Azure Container Apps**, investigating database issues, and resolving production problems are part of my responsibilities.

This phase has taught me to look at my work more carefully. The code I write becomes part of a real business process. Understanding what I am building, testing it, and getting to the cause of a problem matter to me.

**In June 2026, I completed my B.Tech with a CGPA of 8.20.** By then, college learning and professional experience were building my foundation together. Java and Spring Boot training, Oracle certification, and practising **more than 200 DSA problems** also helped strengthen my skills.

Today, my direction is **Java Full Stack Development**. My professional foundation is in backend engineering: Java, Spring Boot, APIs, microservices, Kafka, and databases. My frontend skills include building user-facing experiences with React, JavaScript, HTML, and Tailwind CSS. I enjoy understanding both sides of software and connecting them well.

On the personal side, I value friendship. I put my energy into cricket, enjoy travelling with friends, and stay curious about learning and building new things. I take my work seriously and want to keep room for laughter with the people in my life.

That white house in Bihar, the bamboo school, friendships at Holy Mission, the turn towards Kolkata, Bhopal's Fab Four, hackathon journeys, Pachmarhi, and CloudNexus all have their own place in my life.

When I look back at where I started, I feel how far I have come. I also feel excited about everything there is still to learn, build, and experience.

**I'm Affan Ahmad. This is my story so far. The chapters ahead will have code, cricket, friends, and a few new roads to explore.**

## Bilingual cinematic website specification

### Confirmed direction and current status

Affan has accepted the Hinglish story and requested a 3D website with both Hinglish and English text, spoken narration in both languages, and cinematic sound. The Hinglish wording above is preserved from the accepted draft. The English copy is its faithful adaptation, with the same 35 paragraph segments and factual anchors.

This document contains copy and a production plan. No website code, voice recordings, music files, or deployment has been produced by this document update. No new project repository has yet been supplied. A speech or music generation tool and plugin-discovery action are not currently exposed in this session.

### Product experience

- Begin at the supplied white house in Bihar: white upper walls, red lower walls, veranda, surrounding trees. Use the reference photograph for architecture and atmosphere. The original photo depicts the present-day house; avoid claiming the childhood building was architecturally identical.
- Offer a compact Hinglish / English selector and two clear entry choices: Start with sound; Read silently. Default to Hinglish.
- The language selector changes navigation, buttons, story text, narration, captions, loading notices, and error messages. School, company, project, and personal names stay unchanged.
- Hinglish means Roman-script Hindi mixed naturally with English in visible copy; it does not mean replacing the approved story with formal Hindi. English should sound natural and conversational.
- The narrated experience follows the chronological chapters. Narration timing guides gentle camera movement and readable text reveals. Manual exploration remains available.
- Manual scrolling or jumping to another chapter pauses guided narration and presents Play for that chapter. Do not fight the visitor's scroll with automatic scrolling.
- Silent mode retains the complete story and 3D scenes. Audio is an enhancement; the story must never depend on hearing it.
- Use one original visual world with a consistent scale, palette, lighting approach, and motion language across the life stages.
- Friendship chapters remain warm and platonic. Preserve the respectful tone of the approved Shubhangi passage. Do not create imaginary arguments, betrayals, romance, family hardships, or career claims.
- Do not invent likenesses for friends. Use symbolic props or stylized non-identifiable figures until suitable references are supplied.

### Narration production brief

- Use a warm, conversational Indian male narrator for both languages, with a consistent identity and natural pacing. The voice is a narrator for Affan's first-person story; do not present it as a recording of Affan.
- Aim initially for roughly 130–150 spoken words per minute, slowing slightly for reflective passages and allowing natural pauses. Determine actual duration from the recorded audio.
- Narrate all 35 approved-content segments in each language. Do not turn narration into a shorter, factually different story.
- Keep visible Hinglish in Roman script. Prepare speech-normalized Hindi-script input when required by the selected voice engine, retaining English technical words and names, then listen to the result for natural code-switching.
- Treat personal names as pronunciation items to review by listening: Affan, Shubhangi, Deeksha, Ayan, Banshita, Aarya, and Anshika. Also check Pachmarhi and the complete college name.
- Expand abbreviations for speech where clarity helps: B.Tech, SIRT, ABV-IIITM, SDE 1, REST APIs, JWT, DSA, and CGPA. Keep official spelling in the visible copy. Read 8.20 as eight point two zero.
- Preserve Java Backend Developer as the professional project role and Java Full Stack Development as the broader skill direction.
- Produce audio offline during asset preparation and ship static files with the site. Never expose a narration-service secret in frontend code or require a paid speech request for every visitor.
- Preferred packaging: 11 chapter recordings per language, with a measured paragraph cue map for each chapter. Stable paragraph IDs allow equivalent positions across languages.
- Generate captions and cue timings from the actual finished recordings. Do not invent word-level timings or use estimated durations as proof of synchronization.
- Keep a clean narration master separate from music and ambience so the website can control levels.
- Natural voice recordings are still pending an available production route. Do not ship silently broken narration controls or claim a complete audio build before the files exist and have been listened to.

### Sound production and mixing

- Use one coherent score with chapter variations and a recurring home motif.
- Keep narration, music, and sound effects on separately controlled audio channels.
- Narration should remain clearly intelligible. Begin music roughly 18–24 dB below the voice, then adjust by listening; lower it smoothly while narration is active.
- Use brief fades to prevent clicks and gentle crossfades between background beds. Never play old- and new-language narration simultaneously.
- Use restrained sound effects at meaningful actions or transitions. Limit simultaneous effects and avoid repeating a cue on every small scroll movement.
- Obtain usable original or licensed sound assets and retain their source and license information with the project. Do not assume a track is reusable because it is downloadable.
- Use headphones and ordinary phone/laptop speakers for listening checks. Verify consistent voice volume, clean starts/ends, and no clipping.

### Playback and language-switch behaviour

1. A click or tap on Start with sound initializes playback. Read silently does not initialize audible playback.
2. Use one playback coordinator with explicit idle, loading, playing, paused, ended, and error states. Show Playing only after the media play request succeeds.
3. Keep Play/Pause, Mute, narration volume, music volume, caption visibility, and current-chapter controls accessible by keyboard and touch.
4. Switching language retains the active chapter and equivalent paragraph. Stop the old narration; load the matching segment in the selected language. If playback was active, start from the beginning of the equivalent paragraph once ready; if paused, remain paused.
5. Do not preserve the same numeric timestamp across translated recordings because sentence lengths differ. Match stable paragraph IDs and measured cue boundaries.
6. A chapter jump stops the previous voice before loading the new one. Rapid navigation must not queue multiple narration tracks.
7. Pause sound when the page becomes hidden. When the visitor returns, offer Resume rather than unexpectedly resuming.
8. Remember the language and volume preferences locally. A returning visit still begins without unsolicited audio.
9. Handle denied playback, missing files, buffering, and interrupted requests with a useful retry/play state while keeping the text readable.
10. Guided playback may move to the next chapter after the current narration ends, provided the visitor has not paused or taken over navigation.

### 3D and audio performance requirements

- Reuse a single primary 3D canvas and controlled render loop; share materials and assets where appropriate.
- Load the first scene first. Lazy-load later scenes and the selected language's current/next audio chapter. Do not download both complete voice tracks before the introduction.
- Compress real 3D geometry and textures, cap render resolution on high-density displays, and reduce expensive shadows and post-processing on weaker devices.
- Aim for smooth desktop animation around 60 FPS on the tested target hardware, with adaptive quality on mobile. Report measured results rather than promising a frame rate on every device.
- Maintain readable HTML story content if WebGL initialization fails. Support reduced motion with calm transitions; narration and text remain usable.
- Verify phone layouts, captions, touch controls, playback after a user gesture, language switching during loading/playback, fast chapter jumps, and device memory behaviour.
- This session's remote browser previously failed to create WebGL contexts on reference sites. Final 3D visual/FPS validation must include a hardware-accelerated desktop browser and a real phone; remote DOM checks alone do not establish smoothness.
